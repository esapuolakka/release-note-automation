The morning began quietly, with pale light slipping across the desk and soft rain tapping against the window. Somewhere in the distance, a city was already moving, but here the world felt paused for a moment, waiting for the first decision of the day.

She poured coffee into a chipped mug and opened her laptop, the screen casting a faint blue glow across her face. The to-do list from yesterday stared back at her, largely untouched. She closed the tab and started a new one.

By mid-morning the rain had stopped and the sun was making cautious attempts through the clouds. The office filled gradually, the way it always did, with the rustle of coats and the low murmur of conversations that had started somewhere else and continued here out of habit.

He had been meaning to fix the bug for three weeks. Not because it was hard, but because it required sitting still and thinking carefully, and lately there always seemed to be something louder demanding his attention. Today, he decided, would be different.

The meeting room smelled faintly of dry-erase marker and yesterday's lunch. Someone had written PLACEHOLDER on the whiteboard in large letters and not erased it. Nobody mentioned it. The agenda moved forward.

At four o'clock the light shifted and the office took on a warmer tone, the kind that makes even a Monday feel briefly forgiving. She saved her work, closed three browser tabs she had been meaning to read, and decided they could wait until tomorrow.

The city outside had slowed to its evening pace. Buses moved with more purpose, carrying people homeward. A child counted cracks in the pavement. A dog paused at a lamppost with great interest. The day had been ordinary, which is to say it had been enough.
